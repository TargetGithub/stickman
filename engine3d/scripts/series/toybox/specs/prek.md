# Pre-K video specs (PK-07 to PK-16)

These are the ten pre-K specs for batch 2 of "Toy Box Friends", for ages 3–5. Each one tells the script
writer what the video teaches, what story carries it, and how the engine stages it. The writer uses only
the batch 1 cast, props, shapes, stages, effects, sounds and actions. Robo hosts every episode, Duck is the
sidekick who tries everything first, and one or two animal guests have a clear job. Every episode has a
small problem to solve, a different kind of hook, and one twist that no other episode uses. The pacing is
the same throughout: sentences of 3–7 words, voice speed 0.90–0.95, teach, then repeat, then a quiz with
2–3 s `windup_click` pauses, then a cheer. Timings in each storyline table add up to the length in the
header line. These are specs, not scripts: the dialogue ideas are notes only, and the writer chooses the
final words. Catalog gaps are listed in each spec with a stand-in made from parts that already exist. None
of them blocks writing a script.

### PK-07 · Five Senses Detectives
**Pre-K · Science · 4:10 · playroom**
**Skills:** five_senses_prek · **Standard:** NGSS K-LS1 style and Head Start ELOF P-SCI (observe with the senses), approximate

**1. Big idea.** I find out about the world with my eyes, ears, nose, hands and tongue.

**2. Learning goals.**
- I can name my five senses: see, hear, smell, touch, taste.
- I can point to the body part for each sense.
- I can use one sense to give a clue ("it is red", "it rolls").
- I can say that we only taste food that a grown-up says is OK.

**3. Hook (first 15 s).** A cardboard box drops into the playroom with a soft `block` thump and a big
question mark on top. It wiggles. Robo puts on his "detective" look (a turn to the camera, a slow `nod`)
and says there is a mystery inside. We cannot open it yet. Detectives find clues first. Duck quacks and
wants to peek right now. Robo: "Not yet! We use our senses."

**4. What makes it unique.** It is the only episode built as a whodunnit. Five "suspects" stand in a row
(a blue ball, a yellow star, a red ball, a red cube and an apple). Each sense gives one clue and sends some
suspects away (`hide`) until one is left. Every sense is needed, and the last two senses "check twice"
like real detectives do.

**5. Cast and roles.**

| Who | Job |
|---|---|
| Robo | Chief detective. Asks "What clue do we have?" Cannot taste or smell ("Robots have no nose!"), so he needs helpers. |
| Duck | Junior detective. Eager, quacks at every clue, tastes the apple slice at the end (with a grown-up's OK). |
| Dog (guest) | The nose expert. Sniffs the box in the smell chapter. |
| Bunny (guest) | The ear expert. Turns her head to listen in the hearing chapter. |

**6. Setting and look.** Playroom stage, `soft_day` light. Warm cream floor; the five suspects sit on a low
shelf (a long white `block`) on the right; the mystery box sits on a stool (a `cylinder`) in the centre.
Camera: a calm medium shot, with a slow push-in on the box at each clue and a cut to each body part
"close-up" (a big caption plus Robo pointing). Colour mood: friendly, warm, a little "detective", with the brighter
`studio_bright` light for the final reveal.

**7. Storyline.**

| # | Chapter (scene title) | Time | What happens | Teaching move | Emotional beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The mystery box | 0:00–0:20 (20 s) | Box drops, wiggles; Robo and Duck meet the five suspects. | Show | Curiosity |
| 2 | Look with your eyes | 0:20–0:55 (35 s) | A small hole in the box glows; Duck peeks and sees red. Blue ball and yellow star leave. | Teach, then try | "I found a clue!" |
| 3 | Listen with your ears | 0:55–1:30 (35 s) | Bunny listens while Robo tips the box: a soft roll, not a bonk. Red cube leaves (cubes slide, they do not roll). | Teach, then pause and guess | Concentration, then a giggle |
| 4 | Smell with your nose | 1:30–2:00 (30 s) | Dog sniffs: sweet, like fruit. The red ball leaves (balls do not smell like fruit). | Teach, then try | Surprise ("Dog is the best sniffer!") |
| 5 | Touch with your hands | 2:00–2:35 (35 s) | Robo reaches in: smooth, round, a little stick on top. Only the apple is left. | Teach, then guess | Excitement builds |
| 6 | Taste with your tongue | 2:35–3:05 (30 s) | A grown-up's plate with an apple slice; Duck tastes: crunchy and sweet. Rule: only taste food. | Teach, safety rule | Delight |
| 7 | Case solved! | 3:05–3:30 (25 s) | The box opens; the apple pops out. Confetti. | Show, praise | Triumph |
| 8 | Detective quiz | 3:30–4:00 (30 s) | Which body part? Four quick questions with pauses. | Quiz | Pride |
| 9 | Goodbye, detectives | 4:00–4:10 (10 s) | Chant once more, Robo waves. | Repeat | Warm goodbye |

Total: 250 s = 4:10.

**8. Animation and staging list.**

| Moment | Objects | Actions | Camera |
|---|---|---|---|

| Ears clue | Bunny, box | `move` Bunny in, `turn` bunny#head side to side; `turn` box 20° and back (rolling), `sound` footstep_soft x3 soft; `windup_click` pause; `hide` s_cube | Medium two-shot |

**9. Experiment or hands-on activity.** "Feely sock" at home.
- Steps: a grown-up puts one safe, everyday thing into a clean sock (a spoon, a sponge, a toy car, a
  banana). The child feels it through the sock and says one word about it (soft, hard, bumpy, smooth),
  then guesses. Then sniff a lemon or an orange with eyes closed. Then shake a closed, taped container
  with a few dry beans inside and listen.
- Prediction: "I think it is …" before looking.
- Result: we can often guess without seeing, because other senses give clues.
- Safety (on screen and in the voice): "With a grown-up." Nothing sharp, nothing small enough to swallow;
  the bean shaker stays taped shut. Only taste food a grown-up gives you.

**10. Interaction moments.**
- Call and response: Robo asks "What do we see with?" Kids answer "Eyes!" Repeat for each sense.
- Pause and guess (2–3 s `windup_click`): after each clue, "Which suspect goes home?"
- Move-along: point to your eyes, cup your ears, sniff like Dog, wiggle your fingers, stick out your
  tongue (gently).
- Freeze game in the hearing chapter: "Be very quiet... can you hear the box?"

**11. Song or chant.** "Detective Check" chant, original. A four-beat, tap-your-knees rhythm. Hook line
idea: each line names a body part and its job, ending with "clue, clue, clue!" and a `ding`. It repeats
after every sense chapter with the new sense added (cumulative: one line, then two lines, up to five) and
once in the goodbye. Keep each line to four or five words.

**12. Fun facts.**
- A dog's nose can smell far, far better than a person's nose (Dog chapter; caption "Super sniffer!").
- Rabbits can turn each ear on its own to listen in different directions (Bunny chapter).
- Ducks can see colours, just like you (eyes chapter, said by Duck proudly).

**13. Vocabulary and captions.**

| Word | Style | When |
|---|---|---|
| Five Senses Detectives | title | Hook |
| see / eyes | label | Chapter 2 |
| hear / ears | label | Chapter 3 |
| smell / nose | label | Chapter 4 |
| touch / hands | label | Chapter 5 |
| taste / tongue | label | Chapter 6 |
| clue | subtitle | Every clue |
| 1, 2, 3 | count | Quiz |

**14. Quiz and recap.**

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What do you hear with? | Ears |
| 2 | Dog found a clue with his...? | Nose |
| 3 | How did Duck know the apple was crunchy? | Taste (tongue) |
| 4 | Can we taste a crayon? | No! Only food |

Praise moment: confetti was used in the reveal, so the quiz ends with a `stars` effect (a
`sparkle` sound plus Robo ThumbsUp) and "You are super detectives!"

**15. Sound and music.** Music `playful` at gain 0.24, dipping under each clue. Voice `soft` at 0.93;
Duck lines in `squeaky`; Dog line in `deep`. Effects per chapter: hook `block` and `ding`; eyes
`sparkle`; ears `footstep_soft` (soft rolling), no loud bonks; nose `squeak` soft; touch `ooh`; taste
`giggle`; reveal `magic_sparkle` and `crowd_cheer` at volume 0.6; quiz `windup_click` then `ding`.

**16. Thumbnail and YouTube.**
- Thumbnail moment: Duck peeking at the glowing peephole, the "?" over the box, title caption on screen.
- Title idea: "Five Senses for Kids! 👀👂 Detective Mystery Box with Robo | Toy Box Friends"
- Tags: five senses, five senses for kids, preschool science, see hear smell touch taste, mystery box,
  learn body parts, toddler learning, kids learning video, 3D cartoon for kids, Toy Box Friends.

**17. Catalog gaps.**

| Need | Stand-in from existing parts | List as gap? |
|---|---|---|
| A peephole in the box | A small `circle` with `glow` material on the box's front face | No |
| Apple slice | A small red `sphere` scaled flat, on a flat white `cylinder` plate | Yes, low priority |
| Body-part close-ups (eye, ear, nose, hand, tongue) | Caption plus Robo pointing; animals' heads turn | Yes: simple body-part icons would help |
| Detective hat or magnifying glass | `torus` plus a `cylinder` handle as a magnifier | Yes, nice to have |

**18. Do and don't.**
- Do keep the tongue simple: we taste with our tongue. Do not teach the "tongue map" (it is a myth).
- Do say the senses in the same order every time: see, hear, smell, touch, taste.
- Do make the cube clue fair: show that a cube slides and a ball rolls earlier in the chapter.
- Don't let Duck taste anything but the apple slice; say "a grown-up said yes" on screen.
- Don't use scary detective tropes (no dark rooms, no villains). The mystery is friendly.
- Don't play a loud sound in the hearing chapter; the point is listening carefully.
- Keep each clue to one short sentence: "It is red." "It rolls." "It smells sweet."

### PK-08 · Sink or Float? Bathtub Lab
**Pre-K · Science experiment · 4:20 · playroom (bath corner)**
**Skills:** – (no catalog skill yet) · **Standard:** NGSS K-PS2 style (observe and test objects), Head Start ELOF P-SCI 3 (predict and test), approximate

**1. Big idea.** Some things float on top of the water and some things sink to the bottom, and we can test
to find out.

**2. Learning goals.**
- I can say "float" and "sink" and show them with my thumbs.
- I can make a guess before we test.
- I can watch a test and say what really happened.
- I can say that big things do not always sink.

**3. Hook (first 15 s).** Splash! Duck jumps into a big tub of water and floats, bobbing and quacking.
Robo holds up a red ball and says "Will it float?" A big caption asks the kids to vote: thumbs up for
float, thumbs down for sink. Robo shows both thumbs, and the vote game starts.

**4. What makes it unique.** It is the "vote and test" game show: every item gets a kids' vote, Duck's
vote and Robo's vote before the splash, and Pig keeps score. The twist is the orange: in its peel it
floats, and peeled it sinks. The same fruit gives two answers, so the lesson is "test it, don't just
guess". A big beach ball floating next to a small sinking pebble breaks the idea that big means sink.

**5. Cast and roles.**

| Who | Job |
|---|---|
| Robo | Lab host. Holds up each item, calls the vote, says "Let's test!" |
| Duck | Floats in the tub the whole time; the "float expert". Explains her waterproof feathers at the end. |
| Pig (guest) | Scorekeeper. Stands by two boards, FLOAT and SINK, and hops when an item lands on her side. |
| Frog (guest) | The tester. Jumps up on the tub rim and drops each item in. Loves a splash. |

**6. Setting and look.** Playroom stage, `soft_day` light, a bathroom corner: a big white open tub (stand-in,
see gaps) with teal water and bubbles. Two score boards stand right: a `rectangle` with the word FLOAT
(letter object, blue) above the water line and one with SINK below it (letter object, purple). Camera: a
side view of the tub at water height so kids see both the surface and the bottom, and a cut to a top
view for the splash. Colour mood: clean, bright, bubbly blues and whites.

**7. Storyline.**

| # | Chapter (scene title) | Time | What happens | Teaching move | Emotional beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bath time! | 0:00–0:20 (20 s) | Duck splashes in and floats. Robo asks the first question. | Hook | Fun, splashy |
| 2 | Float and sink | 0:20–0:40 (20 s) | Robo teaches the two words with thumbs; Duck floats (up), a pebble sinks (down) as a demo. | Teach | Clear and calm |
| 3 | Test 1: the ball | 0:40–1:05 (25 s) | Vote, pause, splash: the ball floats. Pig hops by FLOAT. | Try | "Yes!" |
| 4 | Test 2: the spoon | 1:05–1:30 (25 s) | A metal spoon. Vote, splash: it sinks. | Try | Little "oh!" |
| 5 | Test 3: the apple | 1:30–1:55 (25 s) | Many kids think apples sink. Splash: it floats! | Try, surprise | Surprise |
| 6 | Big ball, little pebble | 1:55–2:25 (30 s) | A huge beach ball and a tiny pebble. Robo guesses "big sinks". Splash: the big ball floats, the little pebble sinks. | Try, correct a mistake | Giggles at Robo |
| 7 | The orange trick | 2:25–3:10 (45 s) | The orange floats. Robo peels it (a grown-up job). Vote again. It sinks! The peel is full of tiny air pockets, like a little life jacket. | Experiment | Wow moment |
| 8 | Why does Duck float? | 3:10–3:30 (20 s) | Duck's feathers trap air and her oil keeps the water off. | Teach (fact) | Duck is proud |
| 9 | Lab quiz | 3:30–4:05 (35 s) | Four quick votes with pauses; Pig counts the score. | Quiz | Pride |
| 10 | Bye-bye, bubbles | 4:05–4:20 (15 s) | Bubbles burst out; chant; wave. | Repeat, cheer | Happy |

Total: 260 s = 4:20.

**8. Animation and staging list.**

| Moment | Objects | Actions | Camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duck splashes in | `tub` (white cylinder ring stand-in), `water` (flat teal cylinder, glossy), Duck, `splash` bubbles | `hop` Duck high over the rim then `move` to the water line; `burst` splash with `bubble_pop`; then a gentle `wiggle` loop (bob) | Side view at water height |
| The vote | Robo, caption | `clip` Robo ThumbsUp; `caption` "float?" label; `sound` windup_click 2.5 s | Two-shot Robo and tub |
| Frog drops it in | Frog, item | `move` Frog onto the rim; `hop` Frog; `drop` item `to` the water line (float) with `sound` bubble_pop | Top view for the splash, cut back to side |
| Float result | item | after landing: small `wiggle` (bobbing), stays at the surface; `move` Pig toward FLOAT; `hop` Pig; `caption` "floats" label | Side view |
| Sink result | item | `move` item slowly from the surface `to` the tub floor (dur 1.5, ease quadIn); `sound` bubble_blow soft; `hop` Pig by SINK | Side view, slight push-in |
| Big and little | `beach_ball` (sphere size 1.4, striped stand-in: red sphere), `pebble` (small grey sphere) | `grow` beach ball as it is revealed; `clip` Robo Yes (his wrong guess) then No when he sees it float | Wide to fit both |
| Orange trick | `orange` (orange sphere, matte), `peeled` (smaller, paler orange sphere, hidden) | Orange floats; `hide` orange, `pop` peeled; `caption` "with a grown-up"; vote; `move` peeled down to the floor | Close-up |
| Duck's feathers | Duck, `air` bubbles | `spin` Duck slowly; `burst` small bubbles; `caption` "feathers" | Close on Duck |
| Quiz | four quiz items reuse the props | vote, `windup_click`, `drop` and result moves | Side view |
| Goodbye | all, `party` bubbles | `burst` bubbles; `clip` Robo Wave; `nod` Frog and Pig | Pull out |

**9. Experiment or hands-on activity.** "Sink or Float" at the kitchen sink or bath.
- Steps: fill a bowl or the bath with a little water (a grown-up stays next to you the whole time). Gather
  6 safe things: a plastic toy, a metal spoon, an apple, a leaf, a crayon, an orange. Put a towel down.
- Prediction: sort them into "float" and "sink" piles before testing.
- Test: put each one in gently and watch. Move it to the real pile.
- The orange: test it whole, then a grown-up peels it, and test again.
- Result: an apple and a whole orange float; a spoon sinks; a peeled orange sinks.
- Safety (on screen and in the voice): "With a grown-up, always." Never play near water alone, even a
  small bowl. Wipe spills so nobody slips. A grown-up does the peeling.

**10. Interaction moments.**
- Call and response: "Thumbs up means...?" "Float!" "Thumbs down means...?" "Sink!"
- Pause and guess (2.5 s `windup_click`) before every splash, eight times in all. Keep the pause the same
  each time so it becomes a ritual.
- Move-along: "Float like Duck" (arms out, sway gently); "Sink like a pebble" (slowly crouch to the floor).
- Robo's mistake in chapter 6: kids shout "No, Robo!" when he says big things always sink.

**11. Song or chant.** "Plop or Bob" chant, original. A bouncy two-part call: one line rising for float
("bob, bob, up on top" idea) and one line falling for sink ("down, down, plop" idea), each paired with
the thumbs movement. It plays after each result as a two-second sting (only the matching half), and in
full at the end. Rhythm: four quick claps, then the line.

**12. Fun facts.**
- An apple floats because there is air inside it (chapter 5, caption "air inside!").
- An orange peel has lots of tiny air pockets, so a whole orange floats and a peeled orange sinks
  (chapter 7).
- Ducks spread a special oil from near their tail on their feathers to keep the water off (chapter 8).

**13. Vocabulary and captions.**

| Word | Style | When |
|---|---|---|
| Sink or Float? | title | Hook |
| float | label | Each float result |
| sink | label | Each sink result |
| guess | subtitle | Each vote |
| test | subtitle | Each splash |
| air | label | Apple and orange |
| with a grown-up | subtitle | Peeling and the home activity |
| 1, 2, 3, 4 | count | Pig's score in the quiz |

**14. Quiz and recap.**

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A rubber toy duck: float or sink? | Float |
| 2 | A spoon: float or sink? | Sink |
| 3 | The big beach ball: float or sink? | Float (big things can float) |
| 4 | The peeled orange: float or sink? | Sink |

Recap line: "We guess, then we test." Praise moment: Pig shows the score, bubbles burst, Robo Dance.

**15. Sound and music.** Music `happy` at gain 0.22, ducked under votes. Voice `soft` at 0.93; Duck
`squeaky`; Pig `squeaky`; Frog `deep` for a funny "ribbit" before each drop. Effects: splashes are
`bubble_pop` (never loud); sinking items get a soft `bubble_blow`; votes use `windup_click`; right answers
`ding`; the orange reveal `ooh` then `sparkle`. No loud splashes: pre-K has no loud sudden sounds.

**16. Thumbnail and YouTube.**
- Thumbnail moment: Frog mid-hop on the rim holding the orange over the water, Duck floating, title on
  screen, a big "?" over the orange.
- Title idea: "Sink or Float? 🛁 Easy Science Experiment for Kids | Toy Box Friends"
- Tags: sink or float, sink or float experiment, preschool science, science for kids, bath time learning,
  floating orange, easy experiments for kids, toddler learning, kids learning video, 3D cartoon for kids,
  Toy Box Friends.

**17. Catalog gaps.**

| Need | Stand-in from existing parts | List as gap? |
|---|---|---|
| Bathtub or water tub (open, see-through side) | An open tub cannot be built from closed shapes, so use a low white `box` as the back wall plus a flat glossy teal `cylinder` as the water surface; the front stays open (a cut-away). Sinking items move below the surface to a floor tile. | Yes: a see-through water tub |
| Water surface ripples | A thin teal `torus` that `grow`s and `hide`s at each splash | Yes, nice to have |
| Spoon | A metal `capsule`, long and thin | Yes, low priority |
| Orange (whole and peeled) | Orange `sphere` (glossy) and a smaller, paler orange `sphere` (matte) | No |
| Beach ball | A big `sphere` in red; stripes are not possible | Yes, nice to have |
| Pebble | A small grey `sphere`, matte, scaled flat | No |

**18. Do and don't.**
- Do not say "heavy things sink and light things float". Say "let's test it". The beach ball and the
  peeled orange show why.
- Do not explain density. For pre-K, "air inside helps it float" is enough and is true.
- Do keep water safety in the voice and on screen at the start and end of the home activity.
- Do give every item the same rhythm: show, vote, pause, splash, result, Pig hops. Repetition is the point.
- Don't let Frog or Duck splash loudly or fill the screen with bubbles for long; keep bubbles brief.
- Don't let a living animal "sink"; only objects sink. Duck stays happily floating.
- Keep this episode about floating only. Melting ice and other changes belong to G2-10.

### PK-09 · Magnet Magic: What Sticks?
**Pre-K · Science experiment · 4:05 · studio**
**Skills:** – (no catalog skill yet) · **Standard:** NGSS K-PS2 style (pushes and pulls) leading to 3-PS2-3, Head Start ELOF P-SCI 3, approximate

**1. Big idea.** A magnet pulls some metal things to it, like paper clips, but not wood, plastic or paper.

**2. Learning goals.**
- I can say the word "magnet" and show a magnet "pulling".
- I can sort things into "sticks" and "does not stick".
- I can say that not every shiny metal thing sticks.
- I can say that a magnet can pull through paper.

**3. Hook (first 15 s).** A drum-roll of `windup_click` sounds. Lights go to `studio_bright`. "The Great
Robo-dini!" Robo bows in a magician's hat (stand-in) and waves a red and silver wand over a pile of paper
clips. "Abracadabra!" The clips jump up and stick to the wand. Duck gasps: "Magic!" Robo winks. Is it?

**4. What makes it unique.** It is a magic show that turns into science. Duck discovers the "trick" works
without the magic word (she says "banana" and it still works), so it is not magic, it is a magnet. The
second twist is the foil ball: it is metal and shiny, every kid expects it to stick, and it does not. The
finale is a real "magic" trick kids can do at home: paper clips that dance on a paper plate.

**5. Cast and roles.**

| Who | Job |
|---|---|
| Robo | "The Great Robo-dini", a proud magician who loves a bow. Happily admits the secret. |
| Duck | The audience member who becomes the scientist. Tries the wand with a silly word and finds the secret. |
| Bunny (guest) | Magician's assistant who pops out of the hat and carries each item on a tray. |

**6. Setting and look.** Studio stage with a small "theatre": a red `rectangle` backdrop as curtains, a
low table (a wide `block` on four `cylinder` legs) at centre with a cream tray. `studio_bright` light for
the show, `studio` light for the testing. Camera: front-row view (slightly low, looking up at the table)
with push-ins for each item. Colour mood: magic-show reds and golds, with silver for the metal items.

**7. Storyline.**

| # | Chapter (scene title) | Time | What happens | Teaching move | Emotional beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Great Robo-dini | 0:00–0:20 (20 s) | Drum-roll, hat, Bunny pops out, the paper clips jump to the wand. | Hook | Wonder |

**9. Experiment or hands-on activity.** "What sticks?" hunt, then the dancing clips trick.
- You need: one big magnet (a large fridge magnet or a toy magnet wand), a few paper clips, a paper plate,

- Prediction: sort into "I think it sticks" and "I think it doesn't" before testing.
- Test: touch the magnet to each thing. Then try the fridge door.
- Trick: put paper clips on the paper plate, hold the magnet under it and move it slowly. The clips dance.

- Safety (on screen and in the voice): "With a grown-up." Use one big magnet only. Never use tiny magnets

**9. Experiment or hands-on activity.** "Hide the toy" game at home.
- You need: a cardboard box (or a basket) and one favourite small soft toy.
- Steps: the grown-up says a word and the child puts the toy there: in the box, on the box, under the
  box, behind the box, beside the box. Then swap: the child hides the toy and the grown-up asks "Where
  is it?" The child answers with a position word.
- Then a real hide and seek with the toy around one room: the grown-up gives "warmer" and position clues
  ("it is under something soft").
- Prediction: "Where do you think it is?" before looking.
- Safety: "With a grown-up." Play in one room. Never hide in a fridge, washing machine, car, or anywhere
  that closes; hide behind or beside things, not inside things that shut.

### PK-15 · How Do You Feel?
**Pre-K · Social-emotional · 4:25 · playroom (party decorations)**
**Skills:** listening_prek · **Standard:** CASEL self-awareness and self-management; Head Start ELOF P-SE 7 (recognise and name emotions) and P-SE 8 (manage emotions with support), approximate

**1. Big idea.** All feelings are OK, I can name my feeling, and there are things I can do to feel calm.

**2. Learning goals.**
- I can name five feelings: happy, surprised, sad, angry, worried.
- I can spot a feeling from body clues (a smile, a droopy head, stomping feet).

- I can listen to how a friend feels.

**3. Hook (first 15 s).** A gift drops softly onto the floor with a card on top. Robo reads it: "You are

**9. Experiment or hands-on activity.** "The pepper trick", then "Wash with me".
- Pepper trick (with a grown-up): pour a little water on a plate. A grown-up shakes some ground black
  pepper on top. Touch the middle with a clean dry finger: nothing much happens. Put a drop of dish soap
  on your fingertip and touch the middle again: the pepper zooms to the edges.
- Prediction: "What will the pepper do when the soapy finger touches it?"
- Result: soap pushes the pepper away. The pepper is a pretend "germ"; soap helps wash germs off hands.
  (Keep it as a pretend: real soap lifts germs off skin so water can rinse them away.)
- Wash with me: wet, soap, scrub fronts, backs, between fingers and thumbs while saying the chant twice,
  rinse, dry.
- Safety (on screen and in the voice): "With a grown-up." Keep pepper away from eyes and noses; wash
  hands after; do not drink the water. Use a step stool only with a grown-up holding it.

**18. Do and don't.**
- Do keep this about the body only. No weather, no seasons, no hibernation; those belong to G1-08.
- Do get the numbers right: at least 20 seconds of hand washing; brush 2 minutes, twice a day; a
  pea-sized bit of toothpaste for ages 3 to 6, with a grown-up helping.
- Do keep germs friendly and tiny (small dots, no faces, no "monsters"), and never gross or scary.
- Do not say all germs are bad or that soap "kills" every germ; say washing helps wash germs away.
- Do keep the pepper trick a pretend model ("pretend the pepper is germs"), not a claim about how soap
  works on skin.
- Don't make Pig the "dirty" joke; she loves mud and also loves washing.
- Don't mention specific bedtimes or screens; say "lots of sleep" and show a calm routine.
- Keep the sleep chapter calm: no sudden sounds, slow camera, soft light.
